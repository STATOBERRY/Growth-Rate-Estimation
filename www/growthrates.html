What is CGR vs CAGR?

The Analysis Results table reports three growth rates for every series. They answer the same question, "how fast did it grow each year?", but use the data differently.

Compound Growth Rate (CGR)

CGR fits a log-linear (exponential) trend through all observed years:

ln(y) = a + b·t   ⇒   CGR = (eb − 1) × 100
  • t is the number of years since the first year.
  • CGR p is the p-value of b. Below the chosen level of significance (α), the growth is statistically significant.
  • The slope test assumes independent, homoscedastic normal errors on the log scale.

Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)

CAGR uses only the first and the last year:

CAGR = [ (ylast / yfirst)1 / (years between) − 1 ] × 100

It is quick and intuitive but sensitive to the end points: an unusually good or bad first or last year changes it a lot. When CGR and CAGR disagree strongly, look at the trend plot.

Annual average growth rate (AAGR)

The mean percentage change over adjacent calendar-year pairs with a positive starting value and a nonnegative ending value. Missing years are not bridged.

Why is a rate blank?

  • CGR requires strictly positive values throughout the observed series. CAGR requires positive observed endpoints.
  • CGR needs at least 3 usable years and CAGR at least 2. With fewer, the rate is left blank.
  • If many values are skipped, check the column for zeros typed instead of blanks or for text entries such as "NA" or "-".
