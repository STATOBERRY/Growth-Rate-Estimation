Trend test, instability and decomposition

Trend Fitting

Eligible linear, quadratic, cubic and exponential trends are compared using AICc on the original response scale. The model with the lowest AICc is marked and plotted. Short series may have too few observations to rank some or all models.

Mann-Kendall test and Sen's slope

The Mann-Kendall test asks whether the series rises or falls steadily over time, without assuming a straight line or a normal distribution. Kendall's τ runs from −1 (always falling) to +1 (always rising). If the p-value is below α, the trend is reported as Increasing or Decreasing.

Sen's slope is the median of all pairwise slopes, a robust estimate of the change per year in the series' own units, with a confidence interval at the chosen α. The test and interval assume independent observations. Serial dependence and short records can make inference unreliable.

Instability: CV and Cuddy-Della Valle Index

The coefficient of variation (CV) measures raw year-to-year variability, but a series with a strong trend has a high CV even when it is perfectly steady. The Cuddy-Della Valle Index removes the trend first:

CDVI = CV × √(1 − adjusted R² of the linear trend)

Negative adjusted R² is treated as zero. CV and CDVI require nonnegative observations and a positive mean.

Area-Yield decomposition

Tick Area-Yield decomposition to split the total change in production between the first and last year into three parts. The file needs columns named like Area and Production. Yield is computed as Production / Area, so the three effects always add up to the total change.

ΔP = Y0ΔA  +  A0ΔY  +  ΔAΔY
  • Area effect (Y0ΔA): change due to more or less land at the old yield.
  • Yield effect (A0ΔY): change due to better or worse yield on the old area.
  • Interaction effect (ΔAΔY): the part from both changing together.
